Lab 4: Windows Account Lockout Troubleshooting
Ticket ID: SIM-1002
Ticket Type: Simulated training incident
Category: Account Access / Authentication
Priority: Medium — training scenario
Status: Resolved in simulation

1. Issue Description
An employee reports being unable to sign in to Windows. After basic sign-in checks, the displayed error indicates that the account has been locked due to too many failed sign-in attempts.

2. Impact
One user’s access to company resources is affected in the simulated scenario.

3. Troubleshooting Performed
Verified the username, Caps Lock status, and keyboard layout.
Reviewed the reported error message.
Identified account lockout as the immediate access issue.
Considered possible causes of repeated failed authentication attempts.
Investigated the simulated finding that a phone was attempting to access company email with an outdated password.
Selected the approved recovery approach: update the saved credentials, follow the organization’s account-unlock procedure, and verify successful authentication.
4. Findings
Simulated root cause: A phone repeatedly attempted to connect to company email using an outdated password, triggering repeated failed sign-in attempts.

This finding is part of the training scenario and has not been verified on a live system.

5. Resolution Plan
Update the saved email credentials on the phone using the organization’s approved process.
Verify the user’s identity.
Unlock the account through an authorized procedure, if required.
Confirm that the user can sign in successfully.
Monitor for further failed sign-in attempts.
6. Verification
The correct recovery steps were identified during the simulation. A real account unlock and successful sign-in were not performed or verified.

7. Closure Notes
This simulated ticket demonstrates a structured approach to account-lockout troubleshooting, investigation of stale credentials, secure account recovery, and ticket documentation.

In a real support environment, close the ticket only after the user confirms access has been restored and the issue is resolved.

Windows account troubleshooting

Root-cause investigation
Secure recovery procedures
